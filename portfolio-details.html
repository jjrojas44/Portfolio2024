Case Study:

My disney experience app redesign
Project Overview
  • Project Name: My Disney Experience App Redesign
  • Project Type: Independent UX/UI Redesign Concept
  • Role: UX/UI Designer (Solo Designer)
  • Tools Used: Figma, FigJam, Photoshop, Illustrator, Google Drive/Docs, Coolors
  • Timeline: 2 Weeks
  • Objective: Reimagine the My Disney Experience mobile app with clearer navigation, improved information hierarchy, and a more consistent interface that makes essential vacation-planning features easier to discover and use.
Value Added

This independent redesign explores how a more organized, guest-focused interface could simplify the Walt Disney World planning experience. By emphasizing intuitive navigation, accessible design considerations, and consistent visual components, the concept aims to help guests find important information and navigate key app features with greater confidence.

Problem Statement

The My Disney Experience app serves as a central hub for planning and navigating a Walt Disney World vacation. With features ranging from park maps and attraction information to dining reservations and daily itineraries, guests must navigate a wide range of tools within one mobile experience. This redesign explores how clearer navigation, improved information hierarchy, and more intuitive interactions could make essential features easier to access.

Identified UX Challenges:

  • Complex Navigation: The variety of planning tools and park services creates opportunities to simplify feature discovery and help guests locate frequently used functions.
  • Information Overload: Attractions, dining, reservations, and park information compete for attention, highlighting the importance of clearer content organization and visual hierarchy.
  • Visual Hierarchy: Important actions and supporting information need clear visual distinction so guests can recognize where to focus and what to do next.
  • On-the-Go Accessibility: Guests often access the app while walking through busy parks, making readable typography, recognizable icons, and appropriately sized touch targets important design considerations.

Why a Redesign?

  • Simplify Trip Planning: Explore a more organized interface that helps guests discover park information, dining options, reservations, and itinerary tools.
  • Improve Usability: Refine navigation, content hierarchy, and interface consistency to create clearer pathways between essential app features.
  • Preserve Disney's Identity: Balance Disney's immersive storytelling and recognizable visual style with a modern, practical, and accessibility-conscious mobile interface.

Research Snapshot

Primary User

Daniel James, a 34-year-old married father of one and Jacksonville-based blogger, values organized, stress-free Disney vacations that leave more time for family experiences and storytelling.

Core Problem

Daniel's scenario highlights the challenges of navigating a cluttered app, managing tickets and reservations, and understanding Genie+ and Lightning Lane booking options.

Design Goal

Streamline family vacation planning through clearer reservation pathways, simplified navigation, and more prominent discovery of kid-friendly attractions.

Design Outcomes & Improvements

Organized Trip Planning

Prioritizes tickets, dining reservations, and daily schedules within a clearer information structure to support family planning.

Family-Friendly Discovery

Emphasizes kid-friendly attractions and experiences through more recognizable content categories and homepage priorities.

Clearer Booking Pathways

Explores more understandable attraction reservation options, consistent action labels, and clearer booking information.

User Persona

Daniel is a fictional, scenario-based persona representing a family-focused Disney guest who values efficient planning and memorable experiences. His goals and frustrations helped guide the redesign's focus on organized reservations, simplified interactions, and family-friendly attraction discovery.

Key Insights

  • Planning Complexity: Managing tickets, dining reservations, and daily schedules requires an organized interface that minimizes unnecessary steps.
  • Reservation Clarity: Daniel's frustration with Genie+ and Lightning Lane selections highlights the importance of understandable booking options and clear reservation details.
  • Family-Friendly Discovery: Daniel wants kid-friendly attractions and activities to be more visible on the homepage rather than buried within multiple screens.
  • Time for Family Experiences: As a Disney blogger, Daniel values efficient planning that allows him to focus on enjoying and sharing memorable moments with his family.

How Might We

  • Simplify trip planning so families can organize tickets, dining reservations, and daily schedules with fewer unnecessary interactions?
  • Clarify attraction reservations by presenting Genie+ and Lightning Lane information through more understandable booking pathways?
  • Improve family-friendly discovery by making kid-friendly attractions and experiences more prominent within the homepage?
  • Reduce navigation complexity through clearer information hierarchy and intuitive interactions that support guests planning on the go?

POV Statement

Daniel James, a married father of one and Disney vacation blogger, needs an intuitive way to organize family plans, manage attraction reservations, and discover kid-friendly experiences because he wants to spend less time navigating complicated app features and more time enjoying and sharing memorable Disney moments.

SWOT Analysis

Strengths

  • Recognizable Brand: Disney's storytelling and established visual identity create a familiar foundation for the guest experience.
  • Integrated Planning Tools: Park maps, attraction information, dining reservations, and itinerary features support multiple vacation-planning needs within one app.
  • Real-Time Information: Attraction wait times and park updates provide useful information for guests making decisions throughout their visit.

Weaknesses

  • Navigation Complexity: The wide range of app features creates opportunities to simplify menus and make essential planning tools more discoverable.
  • Reservation Clarity: Daniel's scenario highlights potential confusion when managing attraction bookings, tickets, and dining reservations.
  • Content Prioritization: Family-friendly attractions and frequently used planning features may benefit from greater visibility and clearer organization.

Opportunities

  • Family-Focused Discovery: Highlight kid-friendly attractions and activities through clearer homepage content and navigation categories.
  • Simplified Planning: Explore more organized reservation details, daily schedules, and understandable booking pathways.
  • Personalized Experiences: Consider recommendations and planning tools based on family interests, preferences, and vacation activities.

Threats

  • Alternative Planning Resources: Third-party vacation guides, crowd calendars, and planning apps offer guests additional ways to organize their trips.
  • Changing Park Conditions: Attraction availability, reservation policies, and operational changes can affect guests' plans and information needs.
  • Different Digital Comfort Levels: Guests with varying technical experience may find complex planning interactions difficult to navigate.

Affinity Diagram

I used affinity mapping to organize potential improvements into five themes: user experience, booking, real-time information, personalization, and efficiency. This exercise helped connect Daniel's planning frustrations to actionable design opportunities, including simplified navigation, clearer calls to action, streamlined reservations, and more accessible wait-time information. Personalization and custom itineraries emerged as additional opportunities, while consistent branding and intuitive interactions helped define the overall design direction. These groupings provided a structured foundation for prioritizing usability and creating a more organized, family-focused app experience.

Empathy Map

I developed a scenario-based empathy map to explore Daniel's thoughts, expectations, and frustrations while planning and navigating a Disney vacation. The exercise highlighted concerns about complex navigation, unclear booking instructions, difficulty modifying reservations, and keeping track of accurate attraction wait times and dining availability. It also revealed opportunities for more accessible real-time updates, personalized recommendations, and flexible planning tools. These insights helped shape my design priorities around intuitive navigation, clearer reservation workflows, organized information, and a more adaptable guest experience. The map represents hypothetical user needs rather than findings from direct interviews.

Value Proposition Canvas

I developed a Value Proposition Canvas to connect Daniel's vacation-planning goals, frustrations, and expectations with potential improvements to the My Disney Experience app. The exercise identified three key guest needs: organizing trips efficiently, navigating the parks with confidence, and managing attraction and dining reservations with less friction. I explored solutions including simplified booking and reservation changes, clearer pricing and availability, accessible real-time wait times, and improved park navigation. Personalized recommendations and itinerary suggestions emerged as additional opportunities to enhance the experience. This framework helped me prioritize practical usability improvements while preserving the immersive storytelling and sense of magic associated with Disney.

Card Sorting & Information Architecture

I created a card-sorting exercise to explore a clearer information architecture for the My Disney Experience app. I organized features into seven primary categories: Map, Attractions, Store, Food, Tickets, Lightning Lane, and Profile. Related content was grouped by guest task, including park-specific attractions and dining, ticket types, reservation options, and account information. This structure helped me explore more predictable navigation pathways and prioritize the features Daniel needs when managing family plans, finding kid-friendly experiences, and accessing reservations. The exercise provided a foundation for organizing the app around recognizable guest activities rather than disconnected features.

Competitive Takeaways

  • Universal — Immersive Experiences: Universal's entertainment-focused digital ecosystem highlights the importance of balancing branded experiences with accessible park information and planning tools.
  • Six Flags — Tickets & Passes: Its emphasis on admission products and seasonal offerings reinforces the need to distinguish purchasing options from essential in-park planning features.
  • SeaWorld — Guest Convenience: Ticketing, attraction information, and optional experiences demonstrate the value of organizing services around recognizable guest activities.
  • LEGOLAND — Family-Focused Discovery: Its emphasis on family attractions and themed experiences highlights opportunities to make kid-friendly activities more prominent and easier to explore.

Biggest Design Challenge

  • Simplify Without Removing Functionality: Organize Disney's extensive planning features into intuitive navigation pathways while preserving access to tickets, attractions, dining, and reservations.
  • Prioritize Family Planning: Make essential park information, attraction booking options, and kid-friendly experiences easier to discover for guests like Daniel who manage family schedules on the go.
  • Balance Magic and Usability: Strengthen visual hierarchy, typography, spacing, and calls to action while maintaining Disney's immersive storytelling and recognizable brand identity.

Guest Experience Considerations

  • Navigation & Information Overload: Daniel's scenario highlights how complex screens and scattered information can make it difficult to locate essential planning tools.
  • Reservation Flexibility: Guests may need to adjust attraction and dining reservations when plans change, creating opportunities for clearer instructions and more understandable booking workflows.
  • Real-Time Information & Confidence: Accessible wait times, attraction updates, and dining availability can help guests make informed decisions and adapt their plans throughout the day.

UI Style Guide

Process

  • Audit + Feedback Review: identified tap-count, clarity, and reliability issues.
  • Define “2 taps” goal: anchored layout decisions to speed and discoverability.
  • Restructure Navigation: simplified menus and surfaced top tasks.
  • High-Fidelity UI: improved hierarchy, touch targets, and confirmation feedback.

Wireframe & Design Evolution

sketchs

I began with rough hand sketches to quickly visualize potential layouts and prioritize key guest actions like ride wait times, mobile food ordering, and park maps. This low-stakes step let me experiment with ideas before jumping into wireframes.

Low Fidelity

I translated my sketches into low-fidelity wireframes to quickly map out the layout and user flows without focusing on visuals yet. My main focus here was making sure essential actions — like navigating to ride wait times or mobile ordering — were no more than two taps away at any time.

High Fidelity

Once the layout felt clean and intuitive, I moved to high-fidelity mockups. Here I incorporated Disney’s visual branding — vibrant colors, playful iconography, and clear typography — while still maintaining simplicity and clarity for easy in-park navigation under pressure.

Iteration: First Iteration vs. Final Iteration

What I Learned

  • Balancing Feature Density and Simplicity: It’s critical to streamline navigation and surface the most-used actions (ride reservations, wait times, dining) without overwhelming users with deep menus and cluttered layouts.
  • Self-Guided Research & User Perspective: Without direct user interviews, I relied on persona building, app store feedback, competitor analysis, and my own use of the app to uncover pain points and define priorities.
  • Iterative Wireframing Improves Usability: Early sketches and low-fidelity wireframes allowed me to test multiple approaches for organizing complex features before committing to visual design.
  • Consistency Builds Trust: Aligning iconography, typography, and color schemes with Disney’s brand guidelines reinforced user trust and created a more magical, cohesive experience.
  • Prioritizing Real-Time Data: Surfacing live wait times, Genie+ availability, and dining updates in prominent positions reduces guest stress and improves trip-planning efficiency.
