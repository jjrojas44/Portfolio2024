Case Study:

Halo Infinite Requiem

Halo Infinite: Requiem — Game UI/UX Redesign

Reimagining the Halo Player Experience

An independent game UX/UI concept for a hypothetical sequel to Halo Infinite, exploring how clearer menu navigation, meaningful progression feedback, and cohesive science-fiction visuals could make campaign, multiplayer, and customization features easier to discover.

Developed over two weeks, Requiem explores a player-centered interface that respects Halo's established identity while bringing consistency to its interconnected game systems.

Project Overview

  • Project Name: Halo Infinite: Requiem — Hypothetical Sequel
  • Project Type: Independent Game UX/UI Design Concept
  • Role: UX/UI Designer (Solo Designer)
  • Tools Used: Figma, Adobe Photoshop, Adobe Illustrator, Game UI Database, Dribbble, Behance
  • Timeline: 2 Weeks
  • Project Scope: Main menu, progression, community file sharing, matchmaking, store, and Spartan customization
  • Target Player: Halo players seeking accessible campaign features, competitive multiplayer, clear progression, and meaningful customization
  • Objective: Reimagine the user interface for a hypothetical Halo Infinite sequel, focusing on intuitive menu navigation, improved information hierarchy, consistent visual design, and a more immersive player experience.

Value Added

Halo Infinite: Requiem explores how a refined interface could enhance the Halo experience while preserving the franchise's recognizable visual identity. Through clearer navigation, organized content, and cohesive UI components, this concept aims to make essential game features easier to discover and navigate across the player experience.

The Challenge

Problem Statement

Halo Infinite introduced a modern foundation for the franchise, but aspects of its interface and player experience have also generated criticism surrounding navigation, progression, and customization. These challenges present an opportunity to explore how a hypothetical sequel could offer clearer information architecture, more intuitive interactions, and a more cohesive visual experience while preserving Halo's established identity.

Key UX/UI Challenges Identified

  • Campaign Accessibility: Making mission selection, replay options, and campaign progress easier to discover and navigate.
  • Progression Clarity: Presenting Battle Pass rewards, unlock requirements, and player progression through clearer information hierarchy and more understandable feedback.
  • Navigation & Visual Hierarchy: Reducing unnecessary complexity by organizing menus, improving content prioritization, and establishing consistent interface patterns.
  • Customization Usability: Streamlining access to Spartan customization, cosmetic unlocks, and personalization options through more intuitive menu structures.

Why Explore a Hypothetical Sequel?

  • Envision a modernized Halo interface that respects the franchise's visual identity while applying contemporary game UX/UI principles.
  • Explore clearer navigation across campaign selection, multiplayer features, progression, and customization.
  • Balance Halo's cinematic science-fiction atmosphere with accessibility, usability, and player-centered design.

Research & Discovery

The research phase combined a scenario-based player persona with a competitive analysis of modern shooters to establish priorities for navigation, progression, multiplayer, and customization.

Research Snapshot

Player

Alex, a 28-year-old dedicated Halo veteran, values competitive multiplayer, meaningful progression, immersive storytelling, and community engagement.

Problem

Frustrations with Battle Pass progression, mission replay accessibility, limited environmental variety, and delayed content updates can diminish Alex's overall experience.

Design Opportunity

Explore a more intuitive interface that improves progression visibility, simplifies access to game modes, and supports a cohesive experience across campaign and multiplayer.

Player Persona

Alex represents a dedicated Halo player who values the franchise's science-fiction storytelling, competitive gameplay, and community. This scenario-based persona helps connect player motivations and frustrations to the interface design priorities explored in Requiem.

Key Player Insights

  • Respect Players' Time: Alex wants to spend less time searching through menus and more time enjoying campaign and multiplayer.
  • Meaningful Progression: Rewards, milestones, and unlock requirements should be understandable at a glance.
  • Competitive Multiplayer: Game mode discovery, fireteam management, and community interaction should feel connected.
  • Immersive Storytelling: Campaign discovery and replay access should support the larger Halo experience.

Point of View

Alex, a dedicated Halo veteran, needs a unified interface that makes campaign, multiplayer, customization, and progression easy to navigate because he wants his time spent playing to feel rewarding, engaging, and connected.

How Might We?

  • How might we make Campaign, Multiplayer, Forge, Custom Games, and Customization easier to locate?
  • How might we communicate Battle Pass rewards and progression milestones more clearly?
  • How might we improve discovery of community files, playlists, and multiplayer activities?
  • How might we preserve Halo's science-fiction identity while strengthening interface readability and consistency?

Competitive Analysis

I compared Rainbow Six Siege, Destiny 2, Call of Duty: Black Ops 6, and Battlefield 2042 to explore how modern shooters approach competitive gameplay, player progression, content updates, and multiplayer engagement. The analysis highlighted opportunities to combine clearer progression systems, accessible multiplayer navigation, and engaging game modes while maintaining Halo's distinctive identity.

From Player Insights to Interface Design

The design process translated Alex's needs and the competitive analysis into a series of connected game interfaces: main menu, community file sharing, matchmaking, store, and Spartan customization.

My Design Process

  • Research & Analysis: Defined a player persona and compared modern shooters to identify interface and progression opportunities.
  • Information Organization: Prioritized navigation to Campaign, Multiplayer, Forge, Custom Games, and Customization, alongside visible progression and featured content.
  • Interface Exploration: Designed screens for community browsing, matchmaking, store discovery, and armor customization.
  • Visual Refinement: Improved background integration, color consistency, typography, button visibility, and interface hierarchy across iterations.

Visual Direction

Military-inspired typography, subtle gold accents, cinematic science-fiction backgrounds, and visible controller prompts establish a recognizable Halo-inspired design language. Organized cards and consistent interface patterns help connect otherwise complex game systems.

Wireframes & Design Evolution

The project documents interface concepts and high-fidelity screens rather than a separate set of low-fidelity wireframe images. The following designs show how I organized the game's core systems.

Main Menu & Progression Visibility

The redesigned main menu brings Campaign, Multiplayer, Forge, Custom Games, and Spartan customization into a structured navigation panel. A prominent Battle Pass module displays progression and rank information, while a dedicated news panel highlights featured content. Controller prompts remain visible.

Community & File Sharing

The community interface organizes player-created content into recommended files, popular maps, custom modes, prefabs, and developer selections. Bookmarks, file browsing, and friends' creations provide additional discovery paths.

Matchmaking & Fireteam Management

The matchmaking interface presents multiplayer playlists alongside a visible fireteam roster. Spartan Rank progression, selected activities, and lobby status provide context before entering a match.

Store & Cosmetic Discovery

The redesigned store uses visual cards for armor sets, currency options, promotional offers, and Spartan Passes. Item imagery, prices, availability indicators, and balances help players distinguish purchasable content from owned items.

Spartan Armor Customization

The Augmentation interface combines armor categories, cosmetic selections, and a large Spartan preview. Equipment slots, cosmetic thumbnails, and ownership indicators connect the available options to the character preview.

High-Fidelity Screens

The main menu and Community File Share screens demonstrate the refined visual language: cinematic backgrounds, structured navigation, clear content groupings, and consistent prompts.

Design Outcomes & Intended Impact

Clearer Navigation

Makes major game features and multiplayer playlists more visible through organized menus and recognizable categories.

Progression Visibility

Gives Battle Pass information, ranks, and rewards a clearer place in the player interface.

Cohesive Player Experience

Connects community content, matchmaking, store discovery, and Spartan customization through consistent visual patterns.

Reflection & What I Learned

  • Designing for Complex Game Systems: Reimagining Halo's progression, matchmaking, and customization interfaces taught me how to organize interconnected features through clearer navigation, consistent layouts, and accessible information without sacrificing the depth players expect.
  • Translating Research into Design Decisions: Developing a player persona and comparing Rainbow Six Siege, Destiny 2, Call of Duty: Black Ops 6, and Battlefield 2042 helped me identify opportunities to improve content discovery, progression visibility, and multiplayer navigation while maintaining Halo's unique identity.
  • Strengthening Visual Hierarchy and Consistency: Designing interfaces for the main menu, Store, Community File Share, Matchmaking, and Spartan Customization reinforced the importance of recognizable navigation patterns, organized content cards, readable typography, and consistent controller prompts.
  • Balancing Franchise Identity with Modern UX: Preserving Halo's military-inspired typography, cinematic backgrounds, and science-fiction aesthetic while exploring more structured interfaces taught me how to modernize an established visual language without losing its recognizable character.
  • Designing Around Player Priorities: Considering Alex's goals and frustrations reinforced the importance of respecting players' time, communicating progression clearly, and making frequently used features easier to discover. This project also highlighted the value of future usability testing to validate these design decisions with real players.

Next Steps

Future work could include testing menu discovery with Halo players, evaluating controller navigation, reviewing accessibility, and refining progression and customization flows based on feedback.
